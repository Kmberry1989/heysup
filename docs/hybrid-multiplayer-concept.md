# hey, sup?

*One-page concept — picked from a 20-item checklist, October 8, 2026. Standalone game (not a Selfie Social Society mode). Theme: pure 90s retro. (The gardening theme was removed 2026-10-08.)*

## Logline

A 90s suburban street you share with friends. Deck out your bedroom, raise a TamaGotcha, trade tapes on the block — then hit the arcade at night for rotating party events: kart racing, food-court brawls, and dance parties.

## The plot (slim, but it's there)

You're married, living together in Kokomo. One day you get the bright idea to run a drive-through chilli dog stand straight out of your back bathroom window. The OPEN light flickers on... and a portal rips open in the window and sucks her through. He dives in after her just before it snaps shut.

You land in separate corners of a bizarro doppelgänger world — your street, your town, but stuck in the 90s. The plan: find each other, enjoy the ride, make the time together count, and work on things between you along the way.

## The arch-nemeses: EMO US

A bizarro world has doppelgängers — and yours are the worst. **EMO US**: basically you two, but paler, with long droopy emo bangs hanging over one eye. Same clothes, same faces — just the versions of you who let everything get to them. They think your whole cheerful block party is cringe. They're every bad habit you both swore you'd leave in Kokomo.

The **showdown** is a scheduled event: you and your partner versus EMO US across the minigame collection — a grudge race in Mullet Kart 64, the Mall Brawl finals, a dance battle on the BoomBox. Win, and they have to admit... something. (They never fully admit it. They're emo.)

## The picks that built it

Party minigame collections · Cozy shared worlds · Co-op life sim (90s suburban life) · Casual elimination events · Rhythm/dance parties — made for couples, roommates on the couch, dating, and solo players who just want company without pressure.

## Three pillars

**1. Side-scrolling simplicity.** One axis of movement. Everything readable at a glance. One-thumb controls a grandparent or a toddler can pick up. No camera to fight, no 3D navigation to learn — you walk left and right down the street, and the game comes to you.

**2. Pure 90s.** The game doesn't reference the 90s — it IS the 90s. Dial-up boot, chunky pixels where it counts, the mall food court, the arcade, VHS tracking lines. The parody names aren't jokes layered on top; they're the game's native language.

**3. Endless possibility.** Your bedroom is the persistent backbone; rotating scheduled events keep it fresh forever. New tapes, seasonal mall events, room decor drops, and an event calendar mean there's always a reason to come back — but never a penalty for missing a day.

## The world

A persistent side-scrolling street in the bizarro 90s world — a doppelgänger of home, but everything's a decade off. You land separately: he gets a plain boy's bedroom, she gets a plain girl's bedroom. Bare walls, a bed, a dresser, a window — and the whole game ahead to make them yours. Decorate with progress: posters, VHS shelf, CRT, pog board, lava lamp, all of it.

The **arcade** is the event plaza; the **mall food court** is the hangout. Friends' rooms line the street — visit, leave notes, trade tapes. Other players wander by as ambient company — presence without pressure. And somewhere out there on the block, the other half of your duo is doing the same.

## The loop

- **Day (async, cozy):** decorate your room, check your TamaGotcha, trade VHS tapes and pogs, visit friends' rooms, leave gifts in mailboxes.
- **Night (scheduled events at the arcade):**
  - *Minigame nights* — quick drop-in/drop-out party games, anyone can join mid-round.
  - *Elimination tournaments* — goofy, low-skill-floor knockout brackets. Losing is funny, not punishing.
  - *Dance parties* — synced rhythm play on the BoomBox; judged if you want, just-for-fun if you don't.
- **Progression:** collections (every VHS, full pog sets, plushie shelf), room decor sets, event trophies. Collections, not power.

## Built for pairs

- **Couples & dating:** arcade date nights, duo tournament brackets, co-op minigames tuned for two. The whole plot is a couple finding each other — working on things between you is the quiet main quest.
- **Roommates:** same-screen couch co-op — two kids, one street.
- **Solo:** ambient co-presence. Turn off chat entirely and just hang on the block. Trading and gifts work async through the mailbox.

## Social rules

Everything is opt-in. No global chat pressure — emotes and gifts carry the social weight. No way to trash a room (visitors can leave notes and gifts, never move your stuff). Leaving an event mid-round costs nothing.

## Phone-first UI

**Menus work like a cell phone.** Your character carries a smartphone; opening the menu opens the phone. Home screen, apps, notifications — Room, Events, Mailbox, Friends, Settings are all apps. Everyone already knows how a phone works, so the menu needs zero tutorial.

**Communication works like a phone.** Texting is threaded messages with typing indicators and emoji. Voice calls let you talk while walking the street together. Video calls are FaceTime-style — see each other's reactions during events. The game never invents a chat paradigm; it borrows the one in your pocket.

**Your phone has a TamaGotcha.** The virtual-pet app comes pre-installed: raise a Purrby — a needy, weird little pocket creature. Feed it, play with it, ignore it at your peril (it WILL guilt-trip you with push notifications). Pets can visit each other.

## The 90s are the setting

Every system gets a parody name; every key moment gets a period sound.

**Parody names:**
- MySpace → **MyPlace** (player pages)
- AIM → **BAM** (Buddy Alert Messenger — the texting app)
- AOL → **A-OK Online** (the service you "dial into" when the game boots)
- Windows 95 → **Panes 96** (the boot chime)
- ICQ → **ISeeU** (the "uh-oh!" incoming-message sound)
- Minesweeper → **WEEDSWEEPER** (race minigame — dodge the mimes)
- Winamp → **BoomBox** (the dance-party music player)
- Ask Jeeves → **Ask Beavis** (the help system)
- Clippy → **Squirmy** (a worm assistant designed to look like Clippy, with unwanted tips)
- MapQuest → **BlockQuest** (the street map)
- Napster → **NapTime** (the idle/away status)
- Furby → **Purrby** (the virtual pet species)
- Tamagotchi → **TamaGotcha** (the virtual-pet phone app)
- 3D Maze screensaver → **Cubicle Maze** (the idle/AFK screensaver)
- Blue Screen of Death → **Blue Screen of GAME OVER** (white text on blue: "GAME_OVER.EXE has performed an illegal fun operation")
- "You've Got Mail!" → **"YOU'VE GOT MAIL!"** (mailbox notification, full voice line)
- Mario Kart 64 → **Mullet Kart 64** (kart racing minigame)
- Super Smash Bros → **Mall Brawl** (food-court arena brawler)
- Big Buck Hunter → **Buck Hunt** (co-op light-gun minigame)
- Sonic the Hedgehog → **Toxic the Hedgehog** (speed-race minigame)
- Tetris → **Brick Game** (versus puzzle minigame — the bootleg handheld)
- NBA Jam → **Recess** (dodgeball, kickball, tetherball minis)
- 3D Pinball: Space Cadet → **Arcade Cadet** (pinball minigame)
- Super Mario Bros → **Super Market Bros** (supermarket platformer minigame)
- SkiFree → **SkiFlea** (solo endless skier — dodge the flea-market tables, outrun the yeti)

**Sound moments:**
- Boot: dial-up modem handshake melting into a Panes-96-esque chime.
- Joining a friend's street: the connecting screech.
- Mailbox: "YOU'VE GOT MAIL!"
- Friend online/offline: the BAM door creak open / slam shut.
- Incoming text: the ISeeU "uh-oh!"
- System error: the classic critical-stop *ding*.

## Minigame collection

Multiplayer-first. Every game plays in under 3 minutes, supports 2–4 players, and a loser rejoins the next round instantly.

- **Mullet Kart 64** — kart racing; throw lunch trays, boost on spilled sodas.
- **Mall Brawl** — food-court arena brawler; knock rivals past the Orange Julius.
- **Buck Hunt** — co-op light-gun; don't shoot the mascot.
- **Toxic the Hedgehog** — speed race through the toxic-waste playground; spin through the barrels.
- **Brick Game** — versus puzzle; stack bricks to flood your rival's well.
- **Recess** — dodgeball, kickball, tetherball minis.
- **WEEDSWEEPER** — race: clear your mime field first, or co-op one big field.
- **Arcade Cadet** — pinball; score attack or hotseat.
- **Super Market Bros** — supermarket platformer; bonk cereal boxes, ride the conveyor belts, flag the freezer aisle.
- **Solo/async corner** — SkiFlea for one player; a daily Brick Game puzzle arrives by mail.

## Tech fit

Builds on the Selfie Social Society multiplayer stack already decided: Firebase-only (Auth, Firestore, RTDB for live position). Side-scrolling *simplifies* the netcode versus full 3D — positions are essentially one-dimensional, which makes live sync cheaper and more forgiving on mobile.
